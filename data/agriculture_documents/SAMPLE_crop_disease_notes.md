## Paddy (rice) blast
Rice blast is caused by a fungus. Typical leaf symptoms are spindle-shaped spots with grey or whitish centres and brown margins. Neck blast causes the panicle neck to turn dark and break, leading to chaffy grains. Cool nights, high humidity and heavy nitrogen application favour the disease. Management includes using resistant varieties, balanced nitrogen, avoiding dense planting, and consulting the agriculture officer for fungicide recommendations if the disease spreads.

## Tomato early blight
Early blight causes dark brown spots with concentric rings, often described as a target pattern, first on older lower leaves. Leaves may yellow and drop. Warm, humid weather favours the disease. Management includes crop rotation with non-solanaceous crops, removing infected lower leaves, staking plants to improve air flow, watering at the base rather than overhead, and avoiding wet foliage late in the day.

## Cotton pink bollworm
Pink bollworm larvae feed inside cotton bolls and damage lint and seed. Signs include rosette flowers where petals are stuck together and small entry holes in bolls. Pheromone traps help monitor moth activity. Management includes timely sowing, destroying crop residues after harvest, avoiding very long-duration crops, and following local extension advice on thresholds.

## Chilli leaf curl
Leaf curl in chilli shows upward or downward curling of leaves, reduced leaf size, stunted plants and poor fruit set. It is often associated with sucking pests such as thrips, mites and whiteflies that spread viruses. Management includes healthy nursery raising, rogue out affected plants early, using yellow or blue sticky traps for monitoring, managing weeds around the field, and consulting extension officers for pest control recommendations.

## Groundnut leaf spot
Early and late leaf spot appear as small brown to black spots, often with a yellow halo, on groundnut leaves. Severe infection causes premature leaf fall and yield loss. Crop rotation, removal of volunteer plants and timely sowing reduce disease pressure.
