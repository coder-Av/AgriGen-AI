# SAMPLE DOCUMENT - Integrated Pest Management (IPM) basics
NOTE: This is a short sample written for testing the RAG pipeline. Replace/extend it with official ICAR, ANGRAU, PJTSAU, TNAU and FAO publications before the final demonstration and report.

Integrated Pest Management combines cultural, mechanical, biological and chemical methods. Chemical control is used only as the last option and only when pest or disease levels cross the economic threshold advised by the local agriculture department.

Cultural practices include using certified disease-free seed, crop rotation, removing and destroying infected plant residues, maintaining proper plant spacing for air circulation, and avoiding excess nitrogen fertilizer which makes crops more susceptible to pests and diseases.

Regular field scouting is the foundation of IPM. Inspect plants at least once a week, checking the underside of leaves, stems and fruits. Record the number of affected plants in several random spots across the field rather than judging from the field edge alone.

Biological control includes conserving natural enemies such as spiders, lady beetles and parasitic wasps, and using pheromone traps and light traps to monitor moths. Neem-based products are commonly used as a lower-risk option for sucking pests.

Before any pesticide use, farmers should confirm the problem with the local agriculture officer or Krishi Vigyan Kendra, use only products recommended for that crop, wear protective clothing, and follow the label for dose and waiting period before harvest.
