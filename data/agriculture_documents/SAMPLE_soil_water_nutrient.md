# SAMPLE DOCUMENT - Soil, water and nutrient management basics
NOTE: Sample text for pipeline testing only. Replace with verified ICAR / ANGRAU / TNAU material.

Soil testing every two to three years helps farmers apply fertilizer according to crop need rather than by habit. Samples should be collected from several spots in the field at plough depth, mixed, and sent to the nearest soil testing laboratory. Soil Health Card recommendations should be followed for fertilizer quantities.

Nitrogen deficiency appears as uniform yellowing of older, lower leaves first. Iron deficiency appears as yellowing between the veins of the youngest leaves. Zinc deficiency in paddy is called khaira disease and shows as rusty brown spots on leaves and stunted growth.

Excess nitrogen produces lush soft growth that attracts pests and increases lodging in cereals. Splitting fertilizer into more than one application improves nutrient use efficiency.

Drip irrigation delivers water near the root zone and can save water compared with flood irrigation in crops such as chilli, tomato and cotton. Mulching reduces evaporation and suppresses weeds. Alternate wetting and drying in paddy can reduce water use compared with continuous flooding when managed carefully.

Waterlogging reduces oxygen in the root zone and can cause yellowing and root rot in crops such as cotton, groundnut and red gram, so field drainage should be maintained in the rainy season.
